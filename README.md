## What it does

Hush turns speech into text on your Mac, and once its models are downloaded it works offline.

**One shortcut.** Press ⌃⇧Space in any app and the command bar drops out of the notch: start recording, dictate, transcribe a file or a link, or search every word in your transcripts. You stay in the app you're in. [The command bar](#the-command-bar)

**Calls.** Join a call and Hush offers to record it, from a small panel round your Mac's notch. It records your voice and the call separately and labels who said what. No bot joins the call. Works with Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, Slack, FaceTime and other call apps. [Record calls](#record-calls)

**Files and links.** Drop in audio or video of any length, or paste a link from YouTube, Vimeo, Panopto or a podcast. Pages you sign in to, like your university's lecture recordings, work too. [Transcribe files and links](#transcribe-files-and-links)

**Dictation and captions.** Hold a shortcut, speak and let go: your words appear wherever you're typing, in any app. When you give a talk, Hush shows what you say as live captions on the audience's screen. [Dictation](#dictation) · [Live captions for talks](#live-captions-for-talks)

**Notes.** After every call, file and link, a notes model on your Mac writes a summary, key points and to-dos, each linked to the moment it came from. It translates transcripts and notes too, into 28 languages. [Your library](#your-library)

**Private by design.** No account, no analytics, nothing uploaded. **Settings › Privacy** lists every connection Hush makes and what was sent, and a firewall shows the same. [Privacy](#privacy)

**Ask Claude (MCP).** Off unless you turn it on. If you use Claude, let it read your notes and to-dos, and transcripts if you choose, to answer questions about your calls. What it reads goes to Anthropic, and every read is listed. [Ask Claude about your calls](#ask-claude-about-your-calls-mcp)

## Install

1. [Download Hush.dmg](https://github.com/IrtizaNasar/Hush/releases/latest/download/Hush.dmg).
2. Open it and drag Hush to Applications.
3. Open Hush. Apple has checked it (it's notarised), so macOS opens it without a warning.
4. The Welcome window opens and sets Hush up in seven short steps. [Set up in a few minutes](#set-up-in-a-few-minutes) walks through them.

Hush lives in the menu bar, as a small wave. It shows a Dock icon only while one of its windows is open. The wave gets a red dot while Hush records, a blue one for live captions and dictation, and an orange one while paused.

**Open at login**, in **Settings › General**, keeps Hush ready after a restart, to offer to record calls and answer its shortcuts.

Hush keeps itself up to date: a new version downloads in the background and is installed the next time Hush quits. **Check for Updates…**, in the menu bar icon's menu, looks straight away. **Settings › General › Updates** turns this off.

**3. Let Hush listen.** Two permissions are required, and a third you can skip. Press **Allow** beside each.

- **Microphone**, for your voice in calls, recordings and dictation. Required.
- **System audio**, for the other side of calls, from what your Mac plays. Required.
- **Accessibility**, so dictation can type into the app you're using. Optional: without it, dictated text is copied for you to paste.

Hush hears audio only, never your screen. macOS lists system audio under **Screen & System Audio Recording**, but Hush never needs Screen Recording.

**4. Choose your models.** Hush runs two models on your Mac: a speech model, which turns speech into text, and a notes model, which writes summaries and to-dos. It suggests the ones that suit your Mac. Change them now, or later in **Settings › Models**.

| Speech model | Size | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whisper Large v3 Turbo | 574 MB | Very accurate, and keeps up live in 99 languages |
| Whisper Medium | 539 MB | Nearly as accurate, and lighter: for Macs with 8 GB of RAM |

| Notes model | Size | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Qwen3 4B | 2.5 GB | Quick, and good for most recordings |
| Qwen3 8B | 5.0 GB | Better with long, complicated recordings. Needs 16 GB of RAM |

## Record calls

Join a call and Hush offers to record it. It records your microphone and what your Mac plays separately, so your voice and theirs never mix. On speakers rather than headphones, it takes their voices out of your microphone. No bot joins the call, there's no audio driver to install, and Hush never mutes or changes the microphone in your call app.

Hush recognises Microsoft Teams, Zoom, Webex, Slack, FaceTime, Discord, WhatsApp, Skype, GoTo Meeting and RingCentral. It offers to record calls in Chrome, Safari, Arc, Edge, Firefox and Brave too, such as Google Meet, when the browser uses the microphone and plays sound. Calls through your iPhone show as Phone call.

To record anything else, such as a meeting in a room or a video, use **Start Recording** (⌘R), from the command bar, the menu bar icon or the library. It records your microphone and everything your Mac plays.

### The offer

When an app starts using your microphone, the strip shows its icon and the card asks **Record this call?**

- **Record** starts recording.
- **Not Now** keeps Hush quiet until that app lets go of the microphone.

Nothing is recorded until you say so. To record an app's calls without being asked, add it under **Record automatically** in **Settings › General**. To stop the offers altogether, turn off **Offer to record when a call starts**. The offer also appears at the foot of the library's sidebar.

The card shows the call's name, what Hush is hearing, a timer, and each sentence as it's said, with who said it: You, a name Hush knows the voice of, or Speaker 2. The strip keeps a red dot, a level meter and the timer, so you can leave the card closed and get on with the call.

**Hide for This Session.** Right-click the strip (or use the menu bar icon's menu) and the island leaves the notch for this recording. Hush keeps recording, or captioning, and the menu bar icon keeps the timer, Pause and Stop.

It's for when two apps want the notch. Say you teach with another notch app open, such as Loophole, while Hush captions or records the lecture: both panels sit at the notch, and Hush's covers the other. Hide Hush's for the session and the other app has the notch to itself. It's also handy if you'd simply rather not see the strip.

The island comes back by itself when the call ends, when something needs you (a microphone problem, "still recording?"), to show the recording was saved, and for the next recording. **Show Island** in the menu bar icon's menu brings it back sooner.

The first recording after Hush opens may say it's loading the speech model; everything said meanwhile is kept. The live sentences are a first draft: once the call is over, Hush goes over the whole recording again. [After the call](#after-the-call)

**Stop and Save** (⌘R) ends the recording. The card says **Saved**, with the call's length and what Hush is working on, and offers **Open Transcript** and **Copy Transcript**. Quitting Hush while it records asks first, and keeps what's been recorded.

### When the call ends

<p align="center"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/IrtizaNasar/Hush/main/docs/screenshots/call-ended-card.png?v=053a90d6" alt="The card when the call ends: Call ended, Saving in 7 s, Microsoft Teams stopped using the microphone, with Stop and Save and Keep Recording buttons." width="420"></p>

When the call app lets go of the microphone, the card counts down from 10 seconds, then stops and saves. Press **Keep Recording** to carry on, or **Stop and Save** to save straight away. If you've only muted and the call is still playing, Hush waits until the others have been quiet for 2 minutes. To always stop by hand, turn off **Stop recording when the call ends** in **Settings › General**.

If nobody says anything for 15 minutes, the card asks whether the meeting is over, with **Stop and Save** and **Keep Recording**. Everything so far is kept either way.

### After the call

Hush then finishes the transcript on your Mac: it transcribes the whole recording again for better accuracy, works out who said what, and writes the notes. This takes about 6 minutes per hour of call with Whisper Large v3 Turbo.

You can close everything meanwhile; Hush keeps your Mac awake until it's done. In the library, an amber ring beside the transcript and a banner over its words show how far it has got, with **Stop**.

### When something's wrong

<p align="center"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/IrtizaNasar/Hush/main/docs/screenshots/problem-mic-card.png?v=7f57491e" alt="The card when the microphone isn't allowed: Can't record, Hush can't use the microphone, Allow Hush in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Microphone, with Open Privacy Settings and Dismiss buttons." width="420"></p>

If Hush can't start, the card says why and offers the fix.

| The card says | Press |
| --- | --- |
| Hush can't use the microphone. | **Open Privacy Settings** |
| The … speech model isn't downloaded yet. | **Download Model** |

During a call, it also warns, with a fix, when your microphone is silent, when the lid is closed (your Mac's own microphone is off then), and when it can't hear the other side. If the microphone or the call's sound drops out, Hush reconnects it and keeps recording. If the disk is nearly full, Hush stops and saves before it runs out.

## Live captions for talks

<p align="center"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/IrtizaNasar/Hush/main/docs/screenshots/captions.gif?v=6d855410" alt="A white slide titled Photosynthesis, with three bullet points. **Present with Live Captions** shows what you say as captions on the audience's screen, above Keynote, PowerPoint or Google Slides. Start it from the command bar or the menu bar icon. With two screens, the captions go to the one without the menu bar, such as a projector.

The captions roll up in two lines, and fade when you pause. Hush waits a moment before showing the last words of a sentence, so that words never change once they're on screen.

While you present, the notch panel shows **Live captions**, with **Stop** and **Pause**, and the menu bar wave turns blue. Nothing is saved unless you turn on **Save presentations as transcripts**. Then each talk is kept in the library with its audio, like a meeting.

In **Settings › Presenting**, choose the screen, the text size, centred or left-aligned lines, and the top or foot of the screen. **Preview** shows sample captions on that screen for a few seconds. **Caption what your Mac plays** captions a video you play during the talk too, without mistaking it for your voice.

## Transcribe files and links

### Files

Drop audio or video on the library window, any length, or choose **Transcribe Files…** (⌘O). You can also open a file with Hush from Finder, or drop it on Hush's Dock icon. Files dropped while a folder is open go into that folder.

Hush reads most audio and video files: MP3, M4A, WAV, AIFF, FLAC, MP4 and MOV, and with its link tools downloaded, WebM, MKV, Ogg, Opus, WMA and AVI too.

Files stay where they are. Hush follows a file if you move or rename it, and keeps a small copy of its sound, so you can still play it back and transcribe it again if you delete the original.

The **Files** section of the sidebar shows each file's progress. If the library isn't in front, a notification says when a transcript is ready. Notes are written for files too.

### A watched folder

Choose a folder in **Settings › Storage › Watched folder**, and Hush transcribes the audio and video added to it, saving each transcript beside its file in the format you choose. If the folder already has recordings, Hush asks whether to transcribe those too.

### Links

<p align="center"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/IrtizaNasar/Hush/main/docs/screenshots/link.gif?v=1a9fed97" alt="The Transcribe from Link sheet comes down over the library. Choose **Transcribe from Link…** (⇧⌘L), paste a link from YouTube, Vimeo, Panopto, a podcast or most video pages, and press **Transcribe**. Hush fetches the audio, then transcribes it on your Mac.

For a page only you can see, like your university's Panopto, set **Signed-in pages** to **Sign In First**. A window opens: sign in as usual, open the video, then press **Transcribe This Page**. You stay signed in, in Hush only. If you're signed in to the site in Chrome, Safari, Firefox, Edge or Brave, you can use that browser's sign-in instead. Panopto links choose Sign In First by themselves.

Some sites protect their videos so they can't be downloaded. Play the video on your Mac and use **Start Recording** instead: Hush hears what your Mac plays.

Links need two small free tools (yt-dlp and ffmpeg, 66 MB together). Hush downloads them in setup, or when you first paste a link, and keeps them up to date as sites change. There's nothing to install yourself.

## Your library

<p align="center"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/IrtizaNasar/Hush/main/docs/screenshots/library.png?v=7e2690bd" alt="The Hush library. On the left, the sidebar: Search, the Library section with All, Transcripts and Dictations, the Folders section, and today's transcripts. On the right, the Microsoft Teams call Launch planning: its length, speakers, words and language, the Transcript and Notes tabs, speaker chips for You, Alex and Speaker 2, the conversation in paragraphs, and the player bar at the bottom." width="860"></p>

Everything you record, transcribe and dictate is kept in the library. Open it with **Open Library** (⌘0), from the menu bar icon. The sidebar lists your **Transcripts** and **Dictations** by date, with your folders below.

### Transcripts

A transcript shows who said what. At the top are its length, speakers, words and language, and two tabs: **Transcript** and **Notes** (⌘1 and ⌘2).

<p align="center"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/IrtizaNasar/Hush/main/docs/screenshots/playback.gif?v=a8a5d050" alt="Launch planning playing: the sentence being said is lit in white, the rest dimmed, and the light moves from Alex's opening lines to Your question, Speaker 2's answer and on, as the playhead moves along the waveform in the player bar." width="860"></p>

- **Play it back.** The player bar under the transcript plays the recording: drag along the waveform to move, skip 5 seconds, or play from 0.75× to 2× speed. Space plays and pauses. The sentence playing is lit, and the transcript keeps up with it.
- **Read it your way.** In **View options**, choose **Paragraphs**, like a document; **Conversation**, with you on the right and everyone else on the left; or **Lines**, one sentence a line, each with its time. Show timestamps, hide filler words such as um and uh, and change the text size.
- **Fix a sentence.** Click a sentence and press Return to edit it. Press a number key from 1 to 9 to give it to that speaker. ⌘Z undoes any change.
- **Rename it.** Click the title and type.
- **Transcribe Again.** In **⋯ More**, rebuild the transcript from the audio with the model and words set now. [More below](#transcribe-again).
- **Open in New Window** gives a transcript a window of its own.

### Speakers

<p align="center"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/IrtizaNasar/Hush/main/docs/screenshots/speaker-focus.png?v=88853c59" alt="Launch planning with Alex highlighted: their sentences stand out and everyone else's are dimmed, with a bar under the speaker chips showing Alex, how many sentences she said, and buttons to step through them." width="860"></p>

Hush labels each voice: You, then Speaker 1, Speaker 2 and so on. The chips above the transcript list everyone, with how long each spoke.

- **Name a speaker** by double-clicking their chip. Hush remembers the voice, and uses that name when it hears it again, in any transcript.
- **Highlight a speaker** by clicking their chip. Everyone else dims, and you can step through that person's sentences, or show only theirs.
- **This Is Me**, from a chip's right-click menu, tells Hush that speaker is you and teaches it your voice, so it labels you You in rooms and files too.
- **Look for More Speakers**, in the chips' + menu, listens again when two voices were taken for one.
- **Find Speakers Again**, in the same menu, sorts every line afresh when the transcript shows people who weren't there. Lines you gave a speaker by hand stay theirs, and names stay with their voices.
- **Merge Into**, from a chip's right-click menu, joins two speakers who are really one person.

Your voice and the voices Hush remembers are in **Settings › Voices**. Hush keeps a voiceprint for each, a short summary of how someone sounds rather than a recording, on your Mac.

### Notes and to-dos

<p align="center"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/IrtizaNasar/Hush/main/docs/screenshots/notes.png?v=42cdea25" alt="The Notes tab of Launch planning: a Summary saying the launch moves from the 17th to the 24th because security found two issues in the payment flow, three key points, and a To-do list with Book the security review for Thursday, Send the invite after the call, and Confirm security sign-off this week ticked off, each with the time it came from and who's doing it. At the foot: Written on this Mac by Qwen3 4B." width="860"></p>

After every call, file and link, the notes model writes notes on your Mac: a summary, the key points and a to-do list with who's doing what. They're in the **Notes** tab. The notes say *you* for what you said and name everyone else, and a to-do goes to whoever said they'd do it.

<p align="center"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/IrtizaNasar/Hush/main/docs/screenshots/todo-source.gif?v=43353413" alt="The Notes tab, with the to-do Book the security review for Thursday and its time, 0:27. The transcript opens at that moment, scrolled to the sentence That works. Can we get the review booked for Thursday?, lit in amber, then the notes come back." width="860"></p>

- **Back to the source.** Each to-do shows the time of the moment it came from. Click it, or choose **Show in Transcript**, and the transcript opens at that sentence, lit.
- **Edit anything** in place. Add a point or a to-do, tick to-dos off, or assign them to someone.
- **Copy** copies the notes as Markdown.
- **Kept up to date.** If you edit the transcript later, Hush offers to **Rewrite** the notes. Notes you've edited by hand are never rewritten without asking.
- **A better title.** A transcript still called something like "Zoom call" takes the title its notes suggest. A title you typed is kept.

Notes for an hour-long call take about 2 minutes with Qwen3 4B on an M1 Pro, and about twice that with Qwen3 8B; **Settings › Models** shows the time for your Mac. Notes aren't written for dictations, or for transcripts under 40 words. Turn off **Write notes automatically** in **Settings › Models** to write them only when you press **Write Notes** (⌘↩).

### Note styles

<p align="center"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/IrtizaNasar/Hush/main/docs/screenshots/note-styles.gif?v=60d93ef5" alt="The Notes tab of Launch planning, rewritten three ways: as meeting notes with key points and to-dos; as one-to-one notes under Topics, with Launch date, Blocker and Concern; and in a style of your own, Client update, saying what changed for the client and the dates they need, with one to-do. The style's name shows beside Rewrite at the foot each time." width="860"></p>

The same conversation makes different notes depending on what it was for. Each style fills the same three parts, a summary, points and to-dos, with what matters for that kind of conversation:

| Style | Points | To-dos |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Meeting** | **Key points**: decisions, numbers and dates | What someone agreed or was asked to do |
| **Lecture** | **Key ideas**: definitions, formulas and examples, in the order taught | Readings, exercises and deadlines that were set |
| **Interview** | **Highlights**: answers and opinions, with telling phrases in quotes | Follow-ups someone agreed to |
| **One-to-one** | **Topics**: updates, feedback, blockers and career | Next steps either person agreed to |

Hush picks the style from how the conversation went and says why over the notes, such as "Written as lecture notes, as one voice does most of the talking". **Keep** keeps it. To change it, click the style beside **Rewrite** at the foot of the notes and choose another under **Rewrite As**. ⌘Z brings back the notes it replaced.

<p align="center"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/IrtizaNasar/Hush/main/docs/screenshots/note-styles.png?v=c7b79b64" alt="Settings › Models: Notes, with Write notes automatically, Name transcripts from their notes and the notes models Qwen3 8B and Qwen3 4B; then Note styles, with Style for new notes set to Automatic, a style of your own, Revision, open, saying what the notes should cover: every definition and formula, written out, and anything that sounded like it will be on the exam, and an Add Style button." width="720"></p>

**Make your own** in **Settings › Models › Note styles** with **Add Style**: give it a name and say, in your own words, what the notes should cover, such as "every definition and formula, written out, and anything that sounded like it will be on the exam" for revision, or "what changed for the client, and the dates they need". It appears under **Rewrite As** with the others, and **Style for new notes** can make it the one every transcript starts with.

### Transcribe Again

A transcript can be made again from its recording at any time, from **⋯ More › Transcribe Again…**: with the speech model you use now, and the words in [Vocabulary](#vocabulary). Do it after adding names and terms that came out wrong, or after downloading a more accurate model. Hush finds the speakers again and rewrites the notes, unless you've edited them by hand.

Hush asks first, as the transcript is replaced, edits and speaker names included. While it runs, a banner under the speakers shows how far it is, and **Stop** puts back the transcript as it was. Transcribe Again needs the recording: a file you added is used where it is, or, if it's gone, the small copy of its sound Hush keeps.

## Ask Claude about your calls (MCP)

<p align="center"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/IrtizaNasar/Hush/main/docs/screenshots/settings-integrations.png?v=1c1dc667" alt="Hush Settings on the Integrations tab: Let Claude read your library, switched on; then What sharing means, drawn as Hush on this Mac, an arrow of what you allow when asked to Claude, the app you ask, and an arrow to Anthropic, to answer you; under it, Claude can read: titles and dates, notes and to-dos, transcripts (off), every folder; and never shared: your audio and voices, dictation, recordings still going." width="720"></p>

**Off unless you turn it on.** Until then, Claude can't read anything in Hush. Nothing turns it on but you.

Once it's on, Claude answers from your library: "What did we decide about the launch?", "What's still on my to-do list from this week's calls?", "What was said about pricing last month?"

Turn on **Let Claude read your library** in **Settings › Integrations**, then click **Add to Claude** and reopen Claude (for Claude Code, run the command shown there once). Claude connects to Hush through MCP, the way it connects to other apps, on your Mac only. Turn the switch off and Claude can read nothing, even with Hush added.

- **What Claude can read.** Titles, dates, notes and to-dos; transcripts only if you turn them on. A folder you switch off is hidden from Claude.
- **What it can't.** Your audio, voices and dictation, or a call still recording. It can't record, change or delete anything.
- **Where it goes.** What Claude reads leaves your Mac: Claude sends it to Anthropic to answer you, and keeps it as your Claude account's settings say. Hush itself sends nothing.
- **Every read listed.** **Settings › Privacy** lists each read, with what Claude asked and what Hush gave it, beside the times Hush went online, and marks the day on its 30-day strip. A question that takes a few reads seconds apart is listed as one, and opens to show each.

**Block all internet access**, in **Settings › Privacy**, turns it off and keeps it off until you unblock.

## Vocabulary

<p align="center"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/IrtizaNasar/Hush/main/docs/screenshots/settings-vocabulary.png?v=0d5b08cc" alt="Hush Settings on the Vocabulary tab: a box of your own words, names typed the way they're spelt, and below it the imported lists, Machine learning, Physical computing and Creative coding, each with its number of terms, its first few, and a switch to turn it on or off, under Import a list with an Import… button." width="720"></p>

A speech model writes what it thinks it heard, so names and jargon it doesn't know come out wrong: "ML five JS" for ml5.js, "analog read" for analogRead. Vocabulary is a list of names and terms for Hush to spell your way, capitals included.

In **Settings › Vocabulary**, type your own words, separated by commas or one per line, written as they should appear: PyTorch, p5.js, mRNA. Or import lists: a `.txt`, `.md` or `.csv` file of terms, such as a course's glossary. Switch each list on or off for what you're recording.

Hush gives the speech model the terms each part of a recording seems to need, your own words first, so long lists are fine. Vocabulary works for calls, talks, dictation and files. It helps with spelling, but can't make the model hear a word that wasn't said, and with long lists on, files take about a quarter longer.

### Sample lists

Three lists, made for teaching, are in [`vocabulary/`](vocabulary/).

| List | Terms | What's in it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [`creative-coding.md`](vocabulary/creative-coding.md) | about 320 | Creative coding tools, the p5.js and Processing vocabulary, techniques, communities and events, and artists |
| [`machine-learning.md`](vocabulary/machine-learning.md) | about 430 | Machine learning, from the foundations and classic models to neural networks, generative models, training, language models, tools, datasets, ethics and people |
| [`physical-computing.md`](vocabulary/physical-computing.md) | about 390 | Physical computing: boards, electronics, components, sensors, actuators, code, communication, making and interaction |

To use one:

1. Open the list above, and download it with GitHub's **Download raw file** button.
2. In Hush, open **Settings › Vocabulary**, press **Import…** and choose the file, or drop the file on the list.
3. Switch on the lists for what you're recording.

### Share a list

Teach or work in a subject with words of its own? Share a list for it, so others can import it too. A list is a Markdown file with one term per line. [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) says how to write one and send it as a pull request.

## Privacy

**Private by design.** No account, no analytics, nothing uploaded. Every connection Hush makes is listed in **Settings › Privacy**, and a firewall shows the same.

Recording, transcribing, telling voices apart, writing notes, translating and searching all happen on your Mac, and work without the internet.

Hush asks macOS for the microphone, system audio and, if you want dictation to type for you, Accessibility. It hears audio only, never your screen. It notices when an app starts using your microphone, but records nothing until you say so.

Hush goes online only to download what it runs, check for updates, and fetch a link you give it.

| What | From | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speech models | Hugging Face: [whisper.cpp's models](https://huggingface.co/ggerganov/whisper.cpp) | Once, in setup or when you choose another. Hush may also fetch Whisper Base (60 MB) by itself, to help with live text |
| Voice detection (885 KB) | Hugging Face: [whisper.cpp's voice detector](https://huggingface.co/ggml-org/whisper-vad) | Once, with the first speech model |

| Hush's updates | This repository's [releases](https://github.com/IrtizaNasar/Hush/releases) and update feed | A check once a day, and the new version when there is one |
| ffmpeg | [Martin Riedl's builds](https://ffmpeg.martin-riedl.de), the Mac builds ffmpeg.org lists | Once |
| The page you paste | That site | When you transcribe from a link |

Each link tool is checked against the checksum its maker publishes, and tried once, before Hush uses it.

**Check for yourself.** **Settings › Privacy** lists every time Hush has gone online: what it was for, what was sent and where to. To check from outside Hush, a firewall app such as [Little Snitch](https://www.obdev.at/products/littlesnitch/) or [LuLu](https://objective-see.org/products/lulu.html) shows each connection any app makes, and can block the rest. Hush needs only these:

- `huggingface.co` and the servers it sends downloads to (`*.huggingface.co`, `*.hf.co`), for models
- `github.com` and `*.githubusercontent.com`, for yt-dlp and Hush's updates
- `ffmpeg.martin-riedl.de`, for ffmpeg
- the site of a link you paste, or sign in to

Block them all once setup is done and Hush still records, transcribes and writes notes; only links and updates stop. For confidential work, turn on **Block all internet access** at the foot of **Settings › Privacy**: Hush makes no connection at all, as if this Mac had no internet, and greys out what needs one (Transcribe from Link, downloads, signing in to pages and update checks). Claude (MCP) is off while it's on, and can't be turned on.

**Claude (MCP).** Off unless you turn it on in **Settings › Integrations**. On, what Claude reads leaves your Mac: Claude sends it to Anthropic to answer you. Hush makes no connection for this: Claude starts a small helper inside Hush, which talks to Hush over a connection on your Mac that only you can open, and Hush opens it only while the switch is on. Nothing listens on the network. Each read is listed in **Settings › Privacy**. Audio, voices and dictation are never shared. See [Ask Claude about your calls](#ask-claude-about-your-calls-mcp).

Your transcripts, recordings and notes are kept in `~/Library/Application Support/Hush`, with the models in its `Models` folder. You can move the library to another folder or drive in **Settings › Storage**; the models stay on your Mac. Voices are kept as voiceprints, not recordings.

To keep less, in **Settings › Storage**:

- **Delete recordings after** a week, a month, a year or a time you choose. Older recordings are deleted by themselves; their transcripts and notes stay. A deleted recording can't be recovered. Files you added are never deleted, only Hush's copy of their sound.
- **Remove Recording Only** deletes one recording now and keeps its text.
- Turn off **Keep recordings** to keep only the text: each recording is deleted once Hush has finished with it.

Deleted transcripts wait in Recently Deleted for 7 days, or the time you choose, then are permanently deleted.

## Questions

**Is Hush free?** Yes. Hush is free to download and use, with no account, no subscription and no limits on how much you transcribe.

**Does Hush work offline?** Yes, once its models are downloaded. Recording, transcribing, dictation, captions, notes and translation all run on your Mac without the internet. Only transcribing from a link needs a connection.

**Is my audio uploaded?** No. Your audio, transcripts and notes stay on your Mac. Hush goes online only to download its models and link tools, check for updates, and fetch a link you give it. See [Privacy](#privacy).

**Can it transcribe a Zoom call?** Yes. Hush offers to record when a call starts in Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Webex, Slack, FaceTime, Discord, WhatsApp, Skype, GoTo Meeting or RingCentral, or in a browser, for Google Meet and the like. It records both sides from your Mac, so no bot joins the call. For anything else, press **Start Recording** (⌘R).

**Do the others know I'm recording?** Hush doesn't join the call or announce itself, so it's up to you to tell them. Let people know when you record them, and ask first where the law requires it.

**Which languages does it understand?** The speech model understands 99 languages. Hush detects the language by itself, or you can choose one of 36 in **Settings › General**. Translation covers 28 languages, among them Spanish, French, German, Arabic, Urdu, Hindi, Chinese and Japanese.

**Can it transcribe YouTube videos?** Yes. Choose **Transcribe from Link…**, paste the link and press **Transcribe**. Vimeo, Panopto, podcasts and most video pages work too, including pages you sign in to. A video that a site protects from downloading can't be fetched; play it and use **Start Recording** instead.

**Does it work on Intel Macs?** No. Hush is for Macs with Apple silicon, M1 or later.

**How much space and memory does it need?** Setup downloads about 3.15 GB with the recommended models; the speech model alone is under 600 MB. A recording takes about 250 MB an hour while it's being made, and much less once saved. Hush suggests models that suit your Mac: Whisper Medium for 8 GB of RAM, and Qwen3 8B only with 16 GB or more.

**Can Hush replace a cloud notetaker like Otter?** For many meetings, yes. It records the call, labels who said what, and writes a summary and to-dos. The difference is where it happens: on your Mac, with no account, no bot in the call and nothing uploaded. It can't share a live transcript with others, or record a call you're not on.

**How do I update or remove Hush?** Hush updates itself; **Check for Updates…** in the menu bar icon's menu looks for a new version straight away. To remove it, quit Hush and move it to the Bin. Your transcripts and models stay in `~/Library/Application Support/Hush`, or wherever you moved your library, until you delete them too.

## Limits

- Hush needs macOS 14.2 or later and a Mac with Apple silicon.
- Transcripts aren't perfect. Names and jargon can come out wrong; Vocabulary helps, but can't make the model hear a word that wasn't said.
- Speaker labels can be wrong, especially when people talk over each other or sound alike. **Look for More Speakers** splits voices taken for one, **Find Speakers Again** sorts them afresh, and you can give any sentence to the right speaker.
- Live sentences are a first draft. The transcript settles after the call, when Hush goes over the whole recording again, which takes about 6 minutes per hour of call.
- Captions trail your voice by about a second and a half, so that words don't change once they're on screen.
- **Hide Hush in screenshots** works for screenshots only. Screen recordings and shared screens still show the notch panel and the command bar, and on macOS 15 and later some screenshots may too. Live captions always show.
- Notes and translation need memory and time. Qwen3 8B needs 16 GB of RAM, and notes for an hour-long call take a few minutes.
- Telling voices apart in a very long recording takes a lot of memory. Hush does it without asking for up to about 6 hours of recording on an 8 GB Mac, 14 on 16 GB and 30 on 32 GB, and asks first beyond that.
- With the lid closed, your Mac's own microphone is off. Use a headset or another microphone.
- A recording pauses when your Mac sleeps.
- Dictation types into other apps only with Accessibility allowed. Without it, the text is copied for you to paste.
- Videos that a site protects from downloading can't be transcribed from a link.
- A recording needs about 250 MB of free space an hour while it's being made.
- Other apps at the notch (Loophole, NotchNook): while Hush's island is up it sits on top; the rest of the time the other app has the notch. To keep the notch clear during a recording, use **Hide for This Session**.
